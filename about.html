About

From molecules to models.
The questions got bigger. So did the toolkit.

Molecules → Membranes → Omics → Populations → Models
Biochemistry · Lipid biology · Mass spectrometry · Data science · ML/AI

José Bozelli is a scientist interested in a deceptively simple problem: we can measure more about the human body than ever before, but knowing what those measurements actually mean is much harder.

That question has taken his work from molecules and cell membranes to biomarkers, omics, population-scale data, and increasingly data science, machine learning, and AI.

Different scales. Different tools. Same instinct: understand the biology, respect the measurement, and don't ask the data to say more than it can.

How I think
01
Start with the biology.

A model finding a pattern doesn't make that pattern biologically meaningful.

02
Respect the measurement.

Every number has a history: sampling, measurement, processing, and normalization determine what it can legitimately tell us.

03
Use the right tool.

Experiment. Mass spectrometry. Statistics. Machine learning. Sophistication isn't the goal. Understanding is.

Today, the questions keep expanding — across scientific consulting, writing, and data science.

The throughline remains the same: understand the measurement, understand the biology beneath it, and be precise about what the evidence allows us to conclude.
